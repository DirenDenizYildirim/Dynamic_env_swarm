## 0. What a TMLR referee grades (read 2026-10-11)

The quotes below came back through the session's web-fetch tool, which passes pages through a
summarizer. Check each one against the page before it is quoted in any document.

- **Acceptance criteria**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html:
  - "Are the claims made in the submission supported by accurate and convincing evidence?" The page calls this the most important criterion.
  - "Any gap between claims and evidence should be addressed by the authors." The tool's paraphrase: by more experiments or by narrowing the claims.
  - "Would some individuals in TMLR's audience be interested in the findings of this paper". The question continues with clear communication.
  - Interest means "there is something to be learned by some researchers in their area from their work".
  - "Problems of presentation should be evaluated in terms of whether they materially impede a reader's ability" (to understand the work and its relevance).
  - "Generally, a reviewer who is unsure as to whether a submission satisfies this criterion should assume that it does."
  - Novelty and significance are explicitly not acceptance criteria.
  - "If a paper fully is AI generated with little or no human involvement, then at least with current AI systems," it "is unlikely to meet this criterion."
- **Reviewer guide**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/reviewer-guide.html:
  - The review form asks "Are the claims made in the submission supported by accurate, convincing and clear evidence?" and "Would at least some individuals in TMLR's audience be interested in knowing the findings of this paper?"
  - Each requested change is marked "critical to securing your recommendation for acceptance" or one that "would simply strengthen the work".
- **Editorial policies**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html:
  - "LLMs may be used as general-purpose assistive tools."
  - Submissions that "appear to be largely LLM-generated will be closely examined for evidence of the issues mentioned previously". Those issues include "fabrication of facts".
- **FAQ**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/faq.html:
  - "the ideas, claims and results found in submissions are human-sourced, not produced by such tools."
  - Tool use is disclosed "as a footnote on the first page".

**Consequence for v2.** TMLR grades the gap between claim and evidence, and clarity. It does not
grade novelty. Narrowing a claim therefore costs nothing at review, and every overreach is a
requested change. The plan below narrows wherever the evidence is thinner than the sentence.
It also flags the places where the scaffold undersells.
