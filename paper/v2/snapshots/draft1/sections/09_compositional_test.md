### 9.1 Design

**Definition 8 (Training protocols and the compositional gap).** Let $J_\theta(\pi)$ be the expected episodic return of joint policy $\pi$ in $\mathcal{M}_\theta$, and let $\theta^*$ be the test configuration, every stressor on at a severity held out of training. Let $\Theta_{\mathrm{iso}}$ be the configurations in which at most one of $\kappa_A$, $\kappa_B$, $\delta$ is nonzero; the fire ($\beta > 0$) burns in all of them. The ISO protocol trains one policy $\pi_{\mathrm{iso}}$ on a uniform mixture of configurations in $\Theta_{\mathrm{iso}}$. The JOINT protocol trains one policy $\pi_{\mathrm{joint}}$ on a mixture whose support includes configurations with every stressor on, at training severities only. The compositional gap is

$$
\Gamma(\theta^*) = J_{\theta^*}(\pi_{\mathrm{joint}}) - J_{\theta^*}(\pi_{\mathrm{iso}}).
$$

The registered test replaces $J$ by an outcome metric's mean over evaluation episodes, on two co-primaries: task completion, the founding primary, on which the founding hypothesis was $\Gamma(\theta^*) > 0$, and survival rate (§3.5). Survival became a co-primary on 2026-08-02<!-- N:DES-12 -->, before the grid, because both couplings' calibration grids had moved survival while their completion effects sat at or below the reproducibility floor (§5–§6).

**Proposition 4 (A transfer bound for a fixed policy).** For a fixed policy and two configurations that share the reward, the difference between its discounted returns is bounded above by a term that grows with how far their transition and observation kernels differ. Every configuration in $\Theta_{\mathrm{iso}}$ differs from $\theta^*$ on the states where both couplings act at once, a collapse-seeded fire within perception-attenuation range of an agent (proof idea in Appendix A).

Proposition 4 bounds what ISO can be *guaranteed* to transfer, not what happens. It does not lower-bound Γ, and compositional generalization can emerge from isolated training; it motivates the experiment, and the experiment decides. Its prediction about ISO's exposure to that region was not tested, because the counter built to measure visits there (§6.5) is co-active only in all-on configurations (§9.2).

The test configuration θ\* has every stressor on at the held-out severity, β 0.49, $\kappa_A$ 0.06, $\kappa_B$ 1.0, δ 1.0<!-- N:CAL-3 --><!-- N:CA-1 --><!-- N:CB-1 --><!-- N:COM-6 -->, and no arm trains at β 0.49<!-- N:CAL-3 --> (Figure 2 in §1). In the table, μ is the share of training episodes carrying a given coupling and p the share carrying both. The active share is the share with at least one behaviourally active stressor, and the fire-only share ν is the rest.

ISO-4, a sensitivity arm outside the confirmatory family, is ISO without its two δ-only components, and the corrected gap Γ₄ is JOINT − ISO-4. All arms share the network and the IPPO hyperparameters (Appendix B<!-- N:DX-2 -->), one shared-parameter policy per training seed<!-- N:ENV-11 --><d-cite key="dewitt2020ippo"></d-cite>, a budget of T\* = 1000<!-- N:DES-13 --> updates of 32,768 environment steps each<!-- N:DX-3 -->, and one evaluation draw, seed 0 × 512 episodes at θ\*<!-- N:DES-13 -->.

Γ is read at matched compute, at the fixed budget T\*<!-- N:METH-6 -->, and convergence is claimed nowhere. The confirmatory family is the two co-primary tests, Šidák-corrected (m = 2<!-- N:DES-13 -->): unpaired normal z tests against $z_\alpha = 2.2365$<!-- N:DES-13 --> on the contrast's standard error from each arm's seed dispersion (§8.2). A null carries its exclusion bound X, the 80 %-power MDE at that α<!-- N:METH-7 -->. Positive cross-arm covariance, the expected sign given the shared seed integers, makes the unpaired test conservative. Floors serve only the check that an effect exceeds rerun noise (§8.4) and, as an upper bound, design-stage power (Appendix D); every interval is conditional on the one evaluation draw.

The design registered k = 40<!-- N:DES-1 --> seeds per arm and a seed rule, capped at 60<!-- N:DES-1 -->, that recomputes k from floors re-measured on the grid's GPU. It gave 46<!-- N:DES-2 --> on an RTX PRO 6000 and 72<!-- N:DES-3 --> on GPU 1, the RTX 5090 that ran the grid (GPU 2 and GPU 3 are two more of that model<!-- N:DX-5 -->; Appendix F). Raising the cap to 72 on 2026-09-22<!-- N:DES-3 --> was a deviation from a registered rule, recorded as one, made before unblinding, and conservative, since added seeds favour neither sign of Γ. The registered k = 60 analysis, on seeds 1–60<!-- N:DES-1 -->, is reported beside it; realized completion power at a 0.03<!-- N:DES-4 --> effect is 60.2 % and 71.6 % at k = 60 and 72<!-- N:DES-6 -->.

JOINT sees each coupling three times as often as ISO<!-- N:DES-7 -->, so Γ mixes per-coupling frequency with composition. A secondary sweep holds μ = 0.5 while p moves from 0 to 0.5<!-- N:DX-28 -->, in the spirit of DBCA<d-cite key="keysers2020"></d-cite>, which the ISO and JOINT endpoints do not follow. On the triangle of training mixtures $\mu = (1 + p - \nu)/2$, so a fixed μ forces ν = p<!-- N:DES-9 -->. Identification arms on the parallel line μ = 0.4, ν = 0.2 + p<!-- N:DES-9 --> identify a plane in p and ν (Appendix H). Secondary arms run k = 20<!-- N:DES-13 --> seeds with δ off, so the p = 0<!-- N:DX-28 --> sweep arm trains on ISO-4's mixture on JOINT's GPU<!-- N:DX-5 -->, and JOINT, unlike every secondary arm, carries δ = 1.0<!-- N:COM-6 -->, inert (§7). The secondary design is **non-verdict-bearing**.

The registered design contains an error, and the bias it creates inflates Γ. Communication denial had been found inert on 2026-07-30<!-- N:V6-2 --> (§7). The design registered on 2026-08-02<!-- N:V6-3 --> kept δ in θ\* for fidelity to the registration and excluded it from the secondary sweeps because it was inert. It still gave ISO two δ-only components of six<!-- N:V6-3 -->. Its review question on δ judged the arms symmetric, on the premise that neither trains on δ = 1.0<!-- N:COM-6 -->, which is false for both, and asked for a second opinion. So a third of ISO's training episodes<!-- N:DES-8 --> are fire-only, which the design never counted: ISO's configured no-stressor share of 0<!-- N:DES-16 --> is true nominally and false behaviourally, and JOINT's active share is 1.5 times ISO's<!-- N:DX-31 -->. A review of the registered design identified this on 2026-08-16<!-- N:V6-1 -->, before the grid, noting that the confirmatory arm applied a different rule to the same fact as the sweeps. The grid began on 2026-09-09<!-- N:DES-12 --> without the correction. The bias, its direction and ISO-4 with its reading rule were registered on 2026-09-18<!-- N:DES-15 -->, mid-grid (120 of 252 runs<!-- N:DES-15 -->) and before unblinding, on the owner's attestation that no outcome mean had been computed or viewed. The composition actually tested is A × B.

Bias (b), the second registered caveat, is differential drift: JOINT − ISO completion drift over the last 100 updates<!-- N:DES-10 -->, a local sensitivity estimate and never a bound. It read +0.01209 per 100 updates on the first floor study (2026-08-10<!-- N:DES-5 -->) and +0.0179 on the 2026-09-08 re-measured floor, both on an RTX PRO 6000, but −0.0083 on GPU 1<!-- N:DES-10 -->. The T = 2000 subsample's differential training-log slopes over updates 500–1000 are +0.00247 (completion) and +0.00718 (survival) per 100 updates<!-- N:DES-11 -->. The sign reverses on the grid GPU, and no direction is claimed.

The registered outcomes, branches A–D of 2026-08-13<!-- N:DES-12 --> and a fifth cell, $A_c$, added before unblinding, are in Appendix E; rejection at $z_\alpha$ on each co-primary fixes which outcome holds, and branch B (survival only) also needs a registered falsifier to pass. The ISO-4 rule states that if Γ rejects on a co-primary and "**Γ₄'s CI on that co-primary includes 0 or its sign is opposite**, the paper states that the effect **does not survive the inert-share correction**". If Γ and Γ₄ are both null, "Γ₄ is quoted as a second exclusion bound". Appendix D quotes every rule with its date.

The grid, which ended on 2026-09-22<!-- N:DES-12 -->, ran under a runner that computes no cross-arm quantity. The pre-unblind rulings of 2026-09-26<!-- N:DX-405 --> fixed the statistic, the sign rule, k = 72<!-- N:DES-3 --> as primary and $A_c$, and unblinding, the first computation of any outcome contrast, followed that day<!-- N:DES-12 --> (Appendix F).

### 9.3 A recommendation and its scope

A comparison of joint and isolated training that leaves the active share free can report an active-share effect as a composition effect. This holds by construction, as JOINT's threefold per-coupling frequency over ISO<!-- N:DES-7 --> does. We make one recommendation, motivated by this result alone: that studies of compositional generalization match the share of training that contains an active stressor before attributing a gain to composition. It was motivated in one environment at one scale, and whether it holds elsewhere is untested. Matching the active share here left JOINT's other differences in place, and the corrected contrast excludes only a large composition effect (§9.2).
