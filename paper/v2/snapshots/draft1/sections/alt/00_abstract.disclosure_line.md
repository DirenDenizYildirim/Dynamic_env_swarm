We report a pre-registered test of whether training one policy on co-occurring stressors beats training it on each in isolation, in the Compound Hostile Environment (CHE), a pure-JAX multi-agent environment where a cellular-automaton fire burns in every episode. Two stressors are coupled to the fire: structural collapse can add ignitions, and smoke attenuates perception by Beer–Lambert transmittance; the third, communication denial, is independent. Both arms train at matched compute and are evaluated at a held-out near-critical severity, with task completion and survival as Šidák-corrected co-primaries. We ran 72<!-- N:DES-3 --> seeds per arm, above the registered cap of 60<!-- N:DES-1 -->, a recorded deviation made before unblinding.

Joint training raises survival by 0.96<!-- N:RES-2 --> points (Šidák-corrected 95 %<!-- N:DES-13 --> CI [0.47, 1.44]<!-- N:RES-2 -->); completion shows no detectable change (−1.73<!-- N:RES-1 --> points, CI [−4.12, +0.66]<!-- N:RES-1 -->), which excludes a joint-training gain larger than 0.66<!-- N:RES-1 --> points. Both differences are sign unstable over the final half<!-- N:RES-11 --> of training: the survival gap opens only in the last fifth<!-- N:RES-12 -->, and completion leans negative from 70 %<!-- N:RES-13 --> of the budget. The survival gain does not survive the inert-share correction. Communication denial is inert under a frozen random-projection encoder at this scale, so the third<!-- N:DES-8 --> of the isolated arm's training episodes that carry only it are fire-only. This is our design error: we kept a stressor already found inert and did not count the fire-only share it created; found before the main training runs, the bias was registered with its correction while they ran, before unblinding. The correction is a 20<!-- N:DES-13 --><!-- N:DES-15 -->-seed sensitivity arm without those episodes, outside the confirmatory family; joint training's survival differs from that arm's by −0.03<!-- N:RES-6 --> points (CI [−0.68, +0.63]<!-- N:RES-6 -->), though it still sees each coupling twice<!-- N:DES-14 --> as often, always combined.

The fire is an ambient hazard that no reward, cost or constraint channel reads; the reward reads only task variables and agent deaths, pricing every death the same. Severity is set relative to a measured percolation threshold, and each stressor is a parameter of one transition or observation kernel, so ablations are bitwise-exact nested models. We describe a measurement protocol with reproducibility floors per metric, per GPU and per artifact. We recommend, from this single environment, that studies of compositional generalization match the share of training with an active stressor before attributing a gain to composition.
