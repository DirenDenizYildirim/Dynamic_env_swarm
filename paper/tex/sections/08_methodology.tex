\section{Methodology: bars come with floors}
\label{sec:method}

Every acceptance threshold in this paper is graded against a measured
reproducibility floor, and several of our own early thresholds failed that
test. We state the rules we ended up with as a methods contribution, each
with the exhibit that forced it.

\paragraph{Floors are per-metric, per-hardware and per-artifact.}
A \emph{floor} is the standard deviation of a metric across identical reruns
of one training run at one seed: the noise the pipeline produces with nothing
varied. A threshold set below the floor cannot pass under the null, so it is
not a test; its output is void, and a void test voids a pass identically.
Floors do not transfer. Re-measuring the Medium-severity floors on a second
card moved the completion floor $2.75\times$ (\todo{verify 0.0145}
$\to$ \todo{verify 0.0399}) while leaving the survival floor unchanged
(\todo{verify 0.0129} vs \todo{verify 0.0130}); neither ``it will be the
same'' nor ``it will differ'' was safe to assume. And a floor must be measured
on the artifact being graded, never on its reference. When we compared a
refactored pipeline against the original, the reference's rerun floor read
zero, and two diagnostic channels that differed between the trees were
promoted to a semantic difference. Direct localization found identical bit
patterns; the refactored tree simply differed from \emph{itself} one time in
four on those channels, inside a floor nobody had measured. An equivalence
claim between $A$ and $B$ needs $A$-vs-$A$ and $B$-vs-$B$.

\paragraph{Contrasts are graded on the contrast's standard error.}
A difference of arm means has $\mathrm{sd}(\Gam) = \sqrt{(\sigma_A^2 +
\sigma_B^2)/k}$; either arm's own $\sigma\sqrt{2/k}$ is a bound, not an
estimate. The two per-arm reads bracket the truth: at our floors they gave
\todo{verify 92.1\%} and \todo{verify 62.8\%} power where the contrast's
power was \todo{verify 76.7\%}. A project that adopts per-artifact floors
\emph{because} arms differ in stability cannot keep a power formula that
assumes they do not.

\paragraph{Design-stage power is an 80\%-power minimum detectable effect at
the family-corrected level.} A bare $2\sigma\sqrt{2/k}$ names the effect at
which one would \emph{just} reject, which is 50\% power. Our seed count was
first ruled on such a figure, without the \v{S}id\'ak correction the same
analysis plan mandated; corrected, the design had \todo{verify 55.6\%} power
against its own motivating effect band. It was caught during registration.
The same slip inside a results section would have produced an underpowered
null presented as a finding.

\paragraph{The floor and the test statistic are different variances.} A floor
is measured from identical reps at one seed; a $k$-seed arm mean varies by
seed and by nondeterminism. The confirmatory test uses the grid's own seed
dispersion. Floors keep two narrower roles: the \emph{beat-reproducibility}
hurdle (does the effect exceed what reruns alone produce) and a design-stage
power basis registered as an upper bound.

\paragraph{Measuring is not grading.} A channel logged in the environment's
info dictionary is \emph{recorded}. It becomes evidence only with membership
in a registered analysis family and a measured floor. No diagnostic is added
to a pre-registered family after registration: that enlarges the family and
inflates the correction. The grid in \S\ref{sec:test} records two
positional-drift channels for free and grades neither, and the paper says
which is which.

\paragraph{Differenced tests are blind to arm-symmetric effects.} A falsifier
of the form ``no cross-arm difference in $X$'' cannot see any $X$ present in
both arms. One such falsifier ran green for three phases while every episode
in both arms drifted to a boundary wall (\S\ref{sec:limits}). Its silence was
evidence about asymmetry only, and we now state that limit wherever such a
test is reported.

\paragraph{Detectability-relative gates diverge on better hardware.} A plateau
criterion of the form $\mathrm{drift}/\mathrm{floor} \le 1$ cannot certify on
a perfect instrument: as the floor shrinks, any nonzero drift fails. On a
quieter card the jointly trained arm's absolute drift \emph{fell} by 12.6\%
and the criterion \emph{failed}, because the floor had halved. We retired the
criterion and replaced it with an explicit fixed-budget estimand plus a
required robustness reading of the contrast's trajectory over the final half
of training (\S\ref{sec:test}). The retired gate's earlier pass is reported as
what it was: an underpowered null.

\paragraph{Reproducibility floors grow with run length, ordered by curriculum
difficulty.} From $T = 500$ to $T = 1000$ updates the isolated-training arm's
completion floor grew \todo{verify 2.1$\times$} and the joint arm's
\todo{verify 5.2$\times$}. Floors measured at one budget do not grade runs at
another.
