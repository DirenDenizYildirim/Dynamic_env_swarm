We introduce the Compound Hostile Environment (\CHE), a pure-JAX multi-agent
reinforcement learning environment in which a cellular-automaton fire is
causally coupled to structural collapse (collapse seeds fire) and to
perception (smoke attenuates observation by Beer--Lambert transmittance),
alongside an independent communication-denial axis. The hazard is an
\emph{ambient survival stressor}: it enters neither the reward nor any cost
or constraint channel, so the only path from hazard to return is through the
transition and observation kernels. Three properties make \CHE{} an
instrument rather than a demonstration. Severity is defined by a
\emph{measured} percolation critical point ($\betahat = 0.500 \pm 0.005$,
reproducing Kesten's exact value for the idealized kernel); every stressor is
a parameter of one kernel, so ablations are \emph{bitwise exact} nested
models; and every acceptance bar in the paper is graded against a measured
reproducibility floor. Calibration yields four findings that stand without
any training comparison: collapse-seeded fire self-limits where the hazard is
worst; compound hostility is rare and bursty; perception decay cannot be
behaviourally suppressed; and inter-agent communication is inert under a
frozen encoder at this scale. We then run a pre-registered, mechanically
blinded test of compositional generalization---whether training on co-active
stressors beats training on the same stressors in isolation at a held-out
severity and matched compute, with $k = 72$ seeds per arm on one card---and
a matched-marginal dose sweep that varies co-occurrence while holding each
stressor's frequency fixed. \gap{one sentence on the outcome, from the branch
file}. All code, configurations, locked constants and the frozen analysis
plan are released.
