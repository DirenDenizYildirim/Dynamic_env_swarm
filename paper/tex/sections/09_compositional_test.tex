\section{The compositional test}
\label{sec:test}

\subsection{Design, registered before any run}

\paragraph{Protocols.} Training severities are Low ($\beta = 0.43$) and High
($\beta = 0.70$); the held-out test point $\thetastar$ is the all-on
configuration at Medium ($\beta = 0.49$), which neither protocol ever trains
on (a test asserts it). \ISO{} is a uniform six-component mixture,
$\{\text{A-only}, \text{B-only}, \delta\text{-only}\} \times \{\text{Low},
\text{High}\}$. \JOINT{} is the two-component mixture $\{\text{all-on}\}
\times \{\text{Low}, \text{High}\}$. Both use the same architecture, the same
optimizer, the same number of environment steps, and are evaluated on the
same 512-episode set at $\thetastar$.

\paragraph{Estimand.} $\Gam = J_{\thetastar}(\JOINT) - J_{\thetastar}(\ISO)$
at \emph{matched budget} $\Tstar = 1000$ updates. We never claim a value at
convergence. Two co-primaries, task completion and survival, form the
confirmatory family with \v{S}id\'ak $m = 2$ ($z_\alpha = 2.2365$). The test
statistic uses the grid's own per-arm seed dispersion in the unpaired
combined form; the interval is conditional on the common evaluation draw and
reflects training-seed variance only. Survival was ruled in as a co-primary
before any confirmatory run, on the calibration-phase observation that both
couplings move survival while completion effects sit at or below the
reproducibility floor (\S\ref{sec:coupB}).

\paragraph{Seeds, and how $k$ was set.} A pre-registered ladder set the seed
count from measured floors, not outcomes: on the card that ran the grid,
fresh floors gave a required $k$ of $72$ for 80\% power against the
motivating completion effect of $0.03$, above the registered cost cap of
$60$. The cap was raised to $72$ before unblinding; raising $k$ moves power
symmetrically and cannot bias the sign. The registered analysis at $k = 60$ is
a prefix of the same seeds and is reported beside the primary with its
realized power. Seeds are the integers $1$ to $72$ in every arm (seed $0$ is
the floor seed and is excluded), and every confirmatory run trained and
evaluated on one card.

\paragraph{Registered outcome branches.} Four framings were registered before
the grid: both co-primaries positive; completion null and survival positive,
claimable only if the registered falsifier $|z_c| + z_\alpha < |z_s|$ holds
(the completion exclusion must be tighter, in standardized units, than the
survival effect); both null, quoted as exclusions with bounds from the
realized dispersion; and a rejecting negative contrast on either co-primary,
which carries no magnitude threshold and is discriminated only by the
contrast's trajectory over the final half of training. The cell ``completion
positive, survival null'' was not registered; had it occurred, the paper
would say so.

\paragraph{Budget robustness.} Eleven checkpoints per confirmatory run
(updates $500$ to $1000$) are retained. $\Gam(t)$ is evaluated at $\thetastar$
over that window after unblinding and read for sign stability. A sign that is
still moving at $\Tstar$ is itself the finding and is reported as one.

\paragraph{Blinding.} The grid runner trains, evaluates, archives and
verifies; it computes no cross-arm quantity, and a test asserts that the
script contains no analysis call. The analysis instrument was written and
tested on synthetic artifacts, committed, and only then run once on the real
artifact with the pipeline frozen at a recorded commit; each real invocation
is logged. The branches, the falsifier, the estimand and the venue were all
fixed before the grid ran.

\paragraph{Registered biases, stated before the result.}
\emph{(a)} One third of \ISO's training episodes carry the $\delta$-only
element, which \S\ref{sec:comms} shows to be inert under our encoder. \ISO{}
therefore effectively trains on $\{A, B, \text{nothing}\}$ while every
\JOINT{} episode carries both live couplings. The bias inflates $\Gam$. A
sensitivity arm \ISOfour{} (the same mixture without the two inert
components, $k = 20$, outside the confirmatory family) is reported beside
$\Gam$ with a registered reading rule: if $\Gam$ rejects on a co-primary and
$\Gam_4 = \JOINT - \ISOfour$ does not, the effect is reported as not
surviving the inert-share correction.
\emph{(b)} The endpoint contrast confounds \emph{dose} with
\emph{composition}: \JOINT{} sees each element with marginal frequency $1$
and \ISO{} with $1/3$. The matched-marginal sweep below is the instrument
that separates them.
\emph{(c)} At $\Tstar$ the arms sit at different points on their learning
curves; the differential drift measured in the launch batch was roughly
$0.012$ per $100$ updates, a local sensitivity estimate and never a bound.

\paragraph{Secondary: the matched-marginal dose sweep.} Five mixtures hold
each element's marginal frequency at exactly $c = 0.5$ while the
co-occurrence fraction takes $p \in \{0, 0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.5\}$; a
second family at $c = 0.4$ with $p \in \{0, 0.2, 0.4\}$ makes marginal and
co-occurrence separately identifiable. Fixing both marginals forces the
no-element share to $1 - 2c + p$, so at $p = 0.5$ half of all episodes
contain no stressor; the simplex forbids holding marginal, co-occurrence and
active-episode share fixed at once, and we report the gradient rather than
hide it. Both families use $k = 20$, are labelled non-verdict-bearing, and
carry an underpowered flag wherever their minimum detectable effect exceeds
the effect band.

\paragraph{Two further registered readouts.} A descriptive $T = 2000$
subsample, four seeds per confirmatory arm, shows whether the differential
drift persists; it is flagged underpowered and enters no decision. An
eval-only readout of every trained checkpoint at High severity is
unconditional and out of family; it is \emph{not} a held-out test, since
\JOINT{} trained on that exact configuration, and its reading rule is
asymmetric for that reason: a null there is an a fortiori exclusion, a
positive is not separable from in-distribution advantage.
