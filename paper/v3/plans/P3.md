## 0. What a TMLR referee grades (pages read 2026-10-14)

These quotes came back through the web-fetch tool, which summarizes pages.
Check each one against the live page before a document quotes it.

- **Acceptance criteria**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html
  - "Are the claims made in the submission supported by accurate and
    convincing evidence?" This is the most important criterion.
  - "Any gap between claims and evidence should be addressed by the
    authors." One way is "for the authors to adjust (reduce) their claims".
  - Interest: "a reviewer who is unsure … should assume that it does".
    Novelty "is not a necessary criterion".
  - A paper can still fail on presentation if readers "cannot determine the
    problem setting or experimental setup".
  - The page says nothing specific about code or about environment papers.
- **Reviewer guide**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/reviewer-guide.html
  - The form asks for claims "supported by accurate, convincing and clear
    evidence", and whether some of TMLR's audience would be interested.
  - Each requested change is marked "critical" or "would simply strengthen".
  - **Review time depends on length:** 2 weeks for "up to 12 pages (of main
    content, before references)", 4 weeks "over 12 pages". These are TMLR
    template pages. How they map to Beyond PDF printed pages is unknown
    (owner question 1).
- **Author guide**, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/author-guide.html
  - "Authors may submit up to 100MB of supplementary material, such data,
    source code or illustrative videos"; it "must be in PDF or ZIP format".
  - "Like submissions, supplementary material must be anonymized."
  - "Submissions may be any length, but a paper's length should be justified
    by its content."
  - This is the venue's own page for the professor's "100 MB" (ledger SUP-2,
    O-4). Whoever closes O-4 should re-read it directly.
- **Beyond PDF instructions**, https://tmlr-beyond-pdf.org/submission-instructions
  - "Please do not use a custom formatting template for your PDF."
  - "Always save a PDF directly from the browser using 'Print to PDF'."
  - The page sets no length limit.

**Consequences for v3:**
1. Narrowing a claim costs nothing at review. Every sentence that outruns
   its row is a requested change.
2. Nothing on TMLR's pages sets a bar for environment papers. The bar below
   comes from this paper's own claims and from what BR asked for (BR §6).
3. Clause "no custom formatting template" means the page budget must be met
   **in the kit's own print layout**. Distill's narrow print column
   (BR:488-489) is a fixed cost, not something to style away.

   `test_locks.py`.
3. **How to run.**
   - The CPU test suite.
   - Training: `python -m che.train.ippo --config … --updates … --seed …`
     (`ippo.py:668-674`).
   - Evaluation: `python -m che.eval.harness --config … --ckpt-dir …`
     (`harness.py:240-244`).
   - Show the exact commands as they run inside the unpacked supplement
     (step 2's builder can supply them).
4. **Compute.**
   - One 1000-update run: DX-4 on one RTX 5090, DX-3 environment steps.

   - The CPU suite time (CLAUDE.md targets about 2 minutes; **NO ROW**:

   (`ippo.py:676-677`), but every reported result uses one instance (ENV-6).
   Say both.
6. **The bitwise-nesting property as a user benefit.** Same keys and a
   switched-off stressor give the nested model's trajectory. So contrasts
   between conditions are common-random-number contrasts. The interaction

### 3.3 V3-E2: Theorem 1's toy against the implemented toy (read-only check, done)

**Result: they do not match.** The code matches its own test; the paper's
closed form for q does not describe the toy that was tested. BR W6 (BR:168-172,
#53, #54) is right about the mismatch, and wrong about one point (item 4
below).

1. **What v2 states** (v2/06:56-62, = `TF:375-394`):
   - the signal arrives with probability e^{−κ_B(d − t + ℓ_f)}: "Beer–Lambert
     over the current distance to the fire, unit smoke density";
   - q = 1 − ∏_{j=ℓ_f}^{d+ℓ_f}(1 − e^{−κ_B j}).
2. **What the toy does** (`che/env/e2c.py:1-60, :273-312`):
   - the signal is the production observation path: the fire cell is
     revealed in the crop with probability τ_t = exp(−κ_B · dist_t · ρ̄_t);
   - dist_t is the **Euclidean** distance to the fire cell (2.83, 2.24, 2.00
     at the toy's d = 2, ℓ_f = 2; `phase4_report.md:146-148`);
   - ρ̄_t is the four-point midpoint mean of a **single-cell, growing** smoke
     field (a scripted fire held Burning, with one smoke step before the
     first look; `e2c.py:46-58`);
   - the test compares measured J\* with ½ + ½q, where
     q = 1 − ∏_t(1 − τ_t) is computed from the measured τ_t
     (`e2c.py:300-312`; the "q_analytic" field), never from the closed form.
3. **The size of the mismatch** (**BUILDER DERIVATION**: `tau_profile` run on
   CPU and checked against the committed `che/bench/results/phase4/m42/e2c_sweep.json`,
   which stores `tau_profile` per κ_B; e.g. τ = 0.7022, 0.6382, 0.6104 at
   κ_B = 0.5):
   - the optical depths dist_t · ρ̄_t are 0.707, 0.898 and 0.987, where the
     stated formula has 4, 3 and 2;
   - at κ_B = 1: toy q = 0.81 (report: 0.8115, `phase4_report.md:67`), but
     the stated closed form gives 0.19;
   - at κ_B = 8: toy q = 0.0046 analytic and 0.0056 Monte Carlo
     (`phase4_report.md:72`); the closed form gives about 1e-7.
4. **Where BR is wrong.** "J\* − ½ = 0.0000" (DX-14) is the **measured**
   value, 0.5000 ± 0.0055. The prediction is 0.5028, and z = −0.51
