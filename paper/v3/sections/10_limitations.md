## 7 Limitations and scope

The largest limitations are stated first in §1 and discussed where they arise: how much composition the test configuration offered (§6.1); the coarse and unevenly applied inert label for communication denial (§4.4); the communication setting that differs between training and test for two arms (§6.2); and one learner, one map and one team size (§1).

Every comparison in the worked example is at matched compute, 1000<!-- N:DES-13 --> updates per run, with no assessment of whether training had plateaued, and says nothing about other budgets (§6.2). The policy has no memory, so it cannot combine repeated looks through persistent smoke, and how Coupling B affects agents that remember is untested. No theorem in this paper says that joint training should win; the hypothesis the worked example tests is empirical. The test configuration is one held-out severity between the two training severities (§6.2). The hazard is simple, with one ignition at reset, a one-step burn and no wind or uneven fuel (§3.1). The only ablation of the death price is narrow, with three seeds<!-- N:ENV-2 -->, 500<!-- N:ENV-2 --> updates and the fire as the only hazard (§3.3). The task needs no coordination, the agents share one set of parameters, and no heuristic or scripted baseline is reported (§1). We compare fixed training distributions and evaluate no adaptive curriculum. Comparisons across GPUs are marked where they occur (§6.3).
