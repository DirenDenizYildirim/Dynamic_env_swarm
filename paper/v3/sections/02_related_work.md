For Coupling B, VULCAN degrades simulated sensing with smoke but has no transmittance model, and JaxWildfire's observations are partial but not attenuated. Our search found no reinforcement-learning environment whose observation kernel attenuates by transmittance. Among the JAX multi-agent benchmarks we surveyed, JaxMARL <d-cite key="rutherford2024jaxmarl"></d-cite>, Multi-Agent Craftax <d-cite key="alomari2025macraftax"></d-cite> and Assistax <d-cite key="hinckeldey2025assistax"></d-cite>, none has an evolving, spreading lethal hazard field or perception attenuated by a hazard.

Domain randomization and unsupervised environment design ask which distribution to train on, the latter adaptively <d-cite key="tobin2017dr,muratore2022drreview,dennis2020paired,jiang2021plr,jiang2021robustplr,parkerholder2022accel"></d-cite>; our worked example compares fixed distributions on a held-out configuration, a zero-shot generalization test <d-cite key="kirk2023zsg"></d-cite>, and evaluates no adaptive curriculum.

The closest earlier study of our question is Gao et al. (2024) <d-cite key="gao2024composition"></d-cite>, who ask, in visual imitation learning for robotic manipulation, whether policies compose environmental factors to succeed on unseen combinations. <!-- SETTLED:v2C1 (2026-10-19, log OWNER QUESTIONS SETTLED: the default stands) (as above: v2's accuracy fix RC1-24 of the owner's "the strategy that covers more combinations does better"; RWV Part 3) -->Comparing data-collection strategies, they find that policies do compose and that a strategy cycling through factors, so that more combinations appear, is generally the most effective; their design, built for data efficiency, does not separate co-occurrence from how often each factor value appears. The DBCA method <d-cite key="keysers2020"></d-cite> builds train and test splits with similar distributions of single elements and different distributions of their combinations; our secondary sweep follows that idea, holding each coupling's frequency fixed while co-occurrence varies (Appendix F). <!-- SETTLED:v2C1 (2026-10-19, log OWNER QUESTIONS SETTLED: the default stands) (as above: v2's accuracy fixes OI-3 of the owner's Erdem and Üre sentences) -->In robust reinforcement learning, Erdem & Üre (2025) <d-cite key="erdem2025mixed"></d-cite> report that single-agent policies trained against an adversary on one modality, observations or actions, remain vulnerable to simultaneous attacks on both (their Tables 3–4). Their attacked runs schedule no unattacked steps, and runs attacked on both modalities carry both budgets, so per-modality frequency, co-occurrence and total budget change together.

A survey of adaptability in multi-agent reinforcement learning (Hu et al., 2025, revised 2026) <d-cite key="hu2025adaptablemarl"></d-cite> calls an environment family highly diagnostic if it "systematically varies" factors such as observability or communication structure "while holding other factors fixed". It mentions compositional generalization but analyses no shift in several factors at once. Wherever we say that no work does something, we mean none found by the search of Appendix H.
